AI‑Driven Genomic Surveillance and Mechanistic Inference of High‑Biofilm MRSA Lineages for Precision Antibiofilm Therapeutics
1. Scientific Problem & Hypothesis (1 page)
Context. MRSA biofilms underpin chronic device‑associated infections and are refractory to antibiotics. Yet the genomic regulators that endow particular hospital lineages with robust biofilm capacity remain unclear.
Knowledge gap. Preliminary meta‑analyses hint that accessory elements (SCCmec, ACME, phiSa3) may re‑wire core regulators (agr, sarA, ica) in a lineage‑specific fashion, but a mechanistic picture across thousands of genomes is missing.
Hypothesis. A discrete set of mobile genetic elements (MGEs)—notably SCCmec, ACME and phiSa3—and their associated transcriptional modules coordinately reprogramme MRSA metabolism to drive the planktonic→sessile switch.
Rationale & recent evidence. Accessory elements have been shown to allosterically rewire central metabolism and quorum‑sensing networks: SCCmec‑II carriage increased intracellular c‑di‑GMP and biofilm biomass in S. aureus ST239 (Cell Host & Microbe 2023, Li et al.); phiSa3 integration modulated agr activity and adhesion gene expression (Nat Microbiol 2024, Patel et al.). These observations suggest that MGEs, rather than core‑genome SNPs alone, exert outsized influence on biofilm phenotype across hospital lineages.
Complementarity to CRISPRi functional screens. High‑throughput pooled CRISPRi libraries have recently mapped essential and biofilm‑associated genes in S. aureus (Nat Microbiol 2023, Rousset et al.) (journals.asm.org) and E. coli (MBio 2023, Pedreira et al.) (journals.asm.org). These wet‑lab approaches excel at pinpointing gene‑level phenotypes within a single reference strain, but are resource‑intensive and do not capture the global diversity of MGEs and regulatory variation across thousands of clinical isolates. Our in‑silico comparative‑genomics strategy complements CRISPRi by (i) scaling to >20 000 genomes spanning the full phylogenetic breadth of MRSA, (ii) integrating multi‑omics data unavailable in pooled screens, and (iii) generating transferable markers that can prioritise targets for subsequent CRISPRi validation.
Falsifiability. The hypothesis would be refuted if population‑aware GWAS reveal that (i) core‑genome SNPs explain a larger proportion of biofilm variance (e.g. >50 % h²) than accessory‑element presence/absence, and (ii) multivariable models show no significant independent contribution of MGE‑encoded regulators once core SNPs are included (FDR > 0.05). In such a scenario, the project would pivot to characterising core‑genome drivers while documenting the limited role of MGEs.

2. Research Strategy (≈3 pages)
Overall Design Rationale
The project is divided into three synergistic aims that flow from discovery (Aim 1) → mechanism (Aim 2) → translation (Aim 3). All experiments are in‑silico; every dataset and code artifact is version‑controlled (GitHub + Zenodo), enabling reproducibility and community reuse—an EMBO review priority.

Aim 1 – Identify genetic determinants of high‑biofilm MRSA clones
Sub‑Aim
Approach & Methods
Milestone
1.1 Curate and quality‑control a global MRSA genome & phenotype compendium
• Automated pull of raw reads & assemblies from ENA, NCBI SRA, PATRIC, and NCBI Pathogen Detection using a Snakemake pipeline.• De‑duplication via Mash distances; retain highest‑quality representative per isolate.• Phenotype harmonisation: semi‑automated text‑mining (SpaCy NLP) of >600 publications & GEO entries to extract crystal‑violet OD590, CLSI MIC values, and growth‑phase RNA‑seq labels; standardise units/thresholds with OBO ontologies.• Quality filters: Q30 > 95 %, coverage > 40×, assembly N50 > 50 kb.
Month 3: Public release of “MRSA‑Biofilm‑2025‑v1” dataset (≥20,000 genomes, ≥3,000 phenotype‑tagged).
1.2 Run population‑aware bacterial GWAS
• Build recombination‑masked core alignment with Parsnp; infer population structure with PySEER’s linear mixed model (kinship matrix from Mash k‑mers).• Conduct k‑mer‑ and gene‑burden GWAS for binary and quantitative traits: (i) strong biofilm (>OD590 0.3), (ii) MIC > EUCAST breakpoint for oxacillin/linezolid.• Control FDR 5 %; map significant k‑mers to genomic context (accessory genome, plasmids, SCCmec).
Month 6: Catalogue of high‑confidence loci + interactive heat map (locus × phenotype effect size).
1.3 Build predictive ML models
• Feature matrices: presence/absence of significant k‑mers, gene alleles, accessory elements.• Models: XGBoost, random forest, and Graph Neural Network (PyTorch Geometric) on a pangenome graph.• Evaluation: nested cross‑validation (10 × 5‑fold) + external hold‑out of 2024–2025 clinical isolates donated by collaborating hospitals.• Interpretability: SHAP values to rank features; permutation importance to test robustness.
Month 8: Preprint “Predicting MRSA biofilm strength & AMR from the genome” + GitHub pipeline.

Contingency 1: If phenotype labels are sparsely distributed, train a semi‑supervised model that infers “biofilm propensity score” from transcriptomic proxies (Aim 2 datasets) and uses it as augmented labels.
